Complete Reference · All Audiences
Master Policy Packet
Autonomy, Capacity, and the Right to Exit

The authoritative reference for all other documents. Develops the full sovereignty model of bodily self-determination, the two-stage capacity assessment protocol, systematic responses to the disability rights, depression, and slippery-slope objections, the forcing-function mechanism and its systemic consequences, model statutory language, and a complete fiscal impact analysis for Canada and the United States across low, medium, and high uptake scenarios.

Law · Legislative Counsel
Legal Foundations
The Legal Basis for Capacity-Based MAiD Access

Establishes constitutional and doctrinal foundations for capacity-based eligibility. Develops three arguments: the Constitutional Pincer (state monopoly on pharmacological exit generates a corresponding duty to provide access); the Informed Refusal Doctrine (the compliance requirement in current MAiD law is anomalous relative to treatment-refusal doctrine); and the Bureaucratic Stress Test (the assessment process generates functional evidence of capacity superior to verbal interview alone).

Elected Officials · Legislative Staff
Legislative Brief
A Legislative Brief on Capacity-Based MAiD

Written for elected officials and legislative staff. Presents the policy problem in plain language, the proposed capacity standard, a review of current jurisdictional models across North America and Europe, the procedural safeguards and how they differ from current law, responses to common legislative objections, and fiscal and political framing. Designed to be readable without prior familiarity with the philosophical literature.

Download PDF
Disability Rights · Medical Ethicists
Advocacy Brief
Across the Disagreement: A Good-Faith Framework

Written for disability rights advocates, medical ethicists, and others with serious concerns about expanded MAiD access. Acknowledges the strongest protectionist arguments in their most rigorous form, demonstrates where current frameworks already fail vulnerable people, and proposes the forcing-function mechanism as genuine common ground between reform advocates and those skeptical of expansion.

Download PDF
Philosophy · Bioethics · Health Policy
Academic Paper
Decisional Capacity as the Criterion for MAiD Eligibility

Written for academic audiences. Develops the full philosophical argument with citation apparatus: the deficiencies of the protectionist model as a principled framework, the sovereignty model grounded in bodily self-determination, the two-stage assessment protocol and its theoretical justification, and systematic responses to philosophical objections including the phenomenological challenge to suffering assessment and the epistemic status of advance directives.

Download PDF
Physicians · Psychiatrists · Clinical Ethics
Clinical Brief
A Clinical and Medical Ethics Brief on Capacity-Based MAiD

Written for physicians, psychiatrists, and clinical ethics boards. Addresses the clinical incoherence of suffering-based criteria as applied in practice, the capacity assessment protocol from a clinical standpoint, the depressive incapacity objection and how the protocol handles it, liability considerations under the proposed framework, and a practical protocol summary designed for clinical implementation.

Implementation Tool
Model Reporting Form
Socioeconomic Driver Reporting — MAiD Case Form

The model mandatory reporting form required under the forcing-function mechanism. Designed to capture the socioeconomic and structural drivers underlying each MAiD request — housing, financial situation, care access, social support — generating the data necessary for political accountability without allowing these factors to function as eligibility criteria or grounds for denial.

Companion Essay · Personal
Autonomy's Exit
Why I Support the Right to Choose Death (And Why That Makes Me More Committed to Life)

A personal essay providing the autobiographical and philosophical foundation for the policy arguments in the packet. Written in a hybrid academic-personal register, it proceeds from the author's own psychiatric history to a political position on bodily sovereignty. Included as context for the philosophical commitments that shape the policy framework — not as a substitute for the arguments it contains. Best read after the policy documents.
